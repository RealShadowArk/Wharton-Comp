## 3. Our strategy: the "floor rule" (Option 3, m = 2)

In plain English:

1. **Floor** = what it would cost *today* to buy bonds that pay all ten
   $50,000 payments.
2. **Cushion** = portfolio value minus floor (the money not needed for the
   payments).
3. **Stocks = 2 × cushion.** The rest goes to bonds. This is recalculated
   every year.
   - After gains, the cushion grows, so we hold more stocks.
   - After losses, money automatically shifts into bonds.
4. The payments are only at risk if stocks fall **about 47% or more in a
   single year** (worse than 2008's -37%). This is called gap risk.

The full model is `context/Laura_Gao_Portfolio_Structure_Model_v4.xlsx`.
It has 20 automated checks, all passing.

## 6. Team conventions (please follow)

- **Label every number:**
  - **Sourced**: from a dated primary source such as an SEC filing, an
    agency or an index provider;
  - **Secondary**: from a news or data site; verify it before relying on it;
  - **Calc**: arithmetic on sourced numbers;
  - **Judgment**: your interpretation.

  Always include the source and the date.
- **Never fill a gap with a guess, a zero or an average.** Leave it blank and
  write MISSING.
- **Research isn't a buy decision.** Priorities like "Research now" aren't
  weights or return forecasts.
- **Returns are nominal USD, net of fees.**
- **Glossary:**
  - **GICS**: the official sector system. Sector → industry group →
    industry → sub-industry.
  - **Peer group**: companies with a similar business model, compared
    against each other.
  - **10-K**: a US company's annual report filed with the SEC (find it on
    EDGAR). **20-F**: the foreign-company equivalent.
