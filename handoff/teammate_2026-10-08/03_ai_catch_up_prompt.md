# Prompt to catch up your AI

**How to use it:**
1. Start a new chat in ChatGPT, Claude or whichever AI you use.
2. Attach `01_project_briefing.md`, `02_your_job.md` and
   `04_research_template_and_sector_summary.md`.
3. Paste everything between the two lines below.

The prompt also includes a short summary, so it still works if your AI can't
read attachments.

---

I just joined a high-school team in the Wharton Global Youth Program
Investment Competition 2026-27. Today is 8 Oct 2026. Please act as my tutor
and research assistant, not as someone who does the work for me. I've
attached three files from my teammate Pat: a project briefing, my job
description, and our research template with a sector summary. Read all three
before answering.

**Short summary in case you can't read the attachments**

- **The client** (fictional), Laura Gao, contributes $300,000 at the start of
  2027 and $150,000 at the start of 2028. She must pay ten fixed $50,000
  payments at the start of each year 2033-2042. 2031-2032 is the riskiest
  window, because a crash then has no time to recover.
- **Our strategy:** a "floor rule". Treasury bonds that cover all ten
  payments are already bought (a 2033-2042 bond ladder at about a 5.24%
  yield). Stocks equal 2 × the money above that floor.
- **Target portfolio:**
  - 33.6% bonds (bought);
  - 18.1% core index fund;
  - 6% sector tilt ETFs;
  - 42.3% individual stocks.
- **My job:** industry research and peer data, to help pick the individual
  stocks. Four industries:
  - Transaction & Payment Processing Services;
  - Pharmaceuticals;
  - Electrical Components & Equipment;
  - Household Products.

  For each, I build a peer group of at least 5 US-listed companies, collect
  raw annual figures from each company's 10-K (revenue, operating income,
  assets, debt, current assets and liabilities, diluted EPS, net income,
  operating cash flow, capex, shares, price), and write a 1-2 page industry
  record.
- **Deadlines:**
  - peer lists by 11 Oct;
  - full data by 14 Oct;
  - industry records by 15 Oct.

**Rules you must follow while helping me**

1. **Don't state financial numbers from memory as facts.** Tell me where to
   find each number (which 10-K, which statement or note) and I'll look it up
   myself. If you do give a figure, label it "unverified" and say why it
   might be wrong.
2. **Every number I record needs a label** (Sourced / Secondary / Calc /
   Judgment), a source and a date. Missing data stays MISSING: never guess,
   use zero or average.
3. **Competition AI rule:** "Work generated by an AI program may not be
   submitted as the team's own." Help me understand, plan, check and find
   sources, but final wording must be mine. Remind me to log each session
   (date, what I asked, what I checked myself).
4. **Research is not a buy decision.** Don't tell me what to buy, how much,
   or what return to expect.
5. **Don't infer Laura's preferences** (sectors, ESG, risk tolerance, Taiwan)
   from her biography.
6. **Keep these separate for every company:** where it's legally based,
   where it's listed, where its revenue comes from, and which currency it
   reports in.

**What I'd like from you now**

1. In about 10 short bullet points, explain back to me:
   - what the project is;
   - what our strategy is;
   - what's already done;
   - exactly what my job is.

   I'll correct anything you got wrong.
2. Explain, as if I'm a beginner:
   - what GICS sectors, industries and sub-industries are;
   - what a peer group is and why we compare companies only within one;
   - where each of my data columns is found in a 10-K (income statement,
     balance sheet, cash-flow statement, cover page).
3. Give me a day-by-day plan from 9 to 15 Oct that hits my three deadlines,
   starting with how to verify each company's GICS classification.
4. List the questions I should ask Pat on our 10-minute call (for example,
   the debt definition and the common price date).

Ask me if anything is unclear before you start step 2.
