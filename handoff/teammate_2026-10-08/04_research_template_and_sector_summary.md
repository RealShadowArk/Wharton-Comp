## Part 1: Pat's research template (sector or industry)

**Title:** [Sector/Industry] in [Country/Region], as of [date]

1. **Scope** (fill in before researching)
   - Name and GICS level (sector, industry group, industry or sub-industry).
   - Country or region, and why this one (where the businesses sell,
     manufacture or borrow).
   - Which of Laura's jobs it could serve (growth before 2033, reserve, or
     flexibility).
   - Research date and who did it.
2. **How the sector is defined**
   - What these businesses sell, and who buys it.
   - The main types of business inside it.
   - Example companies and funds (name, headquarters, listing).
   - Does the headline hide very different businesses?
3. **What drives demand**
   - Why customers buy, with sourced numbers.
   - Link to country research: do GDP, rates or consumer spending actually
     affect it?
   - Which part of the industry drives the headline number.
4. **Can companies keep their profits?**
   - Gross and operating margins, now vs several years.
   - Is today a peak, a low, or typical?
5. **How much funding does the business need?**
   - Capex as a share of revenue.
   - Debt vs cash, and when the debt comes due.
6. **What could change the industry?**
   - Technology, customers, substitutes, supply limits.
   - Policy (tariffs, subsidies, regulation): which changes help and which
     hurt.
7. **What is already priced in?**
   - P/E vs its own history and vs the government bond yield.
   - What growth does the price assume?
8. **The bad scenario**
   - The chain of cause and effect.
   - Worst past years, volatility, beta.
   - What if it happens in 2031-2032?
9. **PESTLE check**
   - One line each for Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and
     Environmental, marked "material" or "not material".
   - Research only the material ones deeply.
10. **Country exposure**
    - Headquarters, listing, revenue by region, manufacturing, and the
      currencies the companies earn and spend in.
    - Which other countries need their own research.
11. **Summary and priority**
    - A one-paragraph summary.
    - The strongest argument against your conclusion.
    - What you don't know yet.
    - Priority label (Research now / Monitor / Insufficient evidence) and why.
    - The next research question.

**Decision check (short):**

| Item | Question |
|---|---|
| Laura's job | Which job, and the cause-and-effect link |
| Facts vs forecasts | The 3-5 numbers the conclusion depends on, each labelled |
| Timing risk | If $100,000 had its worst past year in 2032, how much would be lost? |
| Price check | Expensive vs history and vs Treasuries? What must go right? |
| Diversification | Does a broad index fund already own it? What currency risk does it add? |
| What happens next | Priority; countries to research; handoffs (peer list to M03/M12, bad scenarios to M06) |

**Key reminder from Pat's doc:** research doesn't pick investments. It shows
where risks and opportunities are and what to research next. How much to
invest is decided later.

**Takeaways relevant to your industries:**
- Every sector has lost at least 20% in some calendar year since 1999.
- Health Care and Staples have the smallest worst years. That's why the
  portfolio's placeholder tilts are XLV and XLP.
- Financials look cheapest but have the worst crash history. That's why
  payments (fee income) are worth separating from banks (credit risk).
- About 46% of the S&P 500 is IT plus Communication Services, both driven by
  AI. Industries with a different driver are useful for diversification.
  Electrical equipment is partly AI-linked through power demand, which is
  exactly the question to test.
