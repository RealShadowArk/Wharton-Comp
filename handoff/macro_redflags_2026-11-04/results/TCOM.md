## Ranked flags and what could make the model wrong

1. **JUDGMENT · RED · Hotel margins after the SAMR order.** Hotels have more freedom to set prices and use rivals. If Trip.com needs lower commissions or more incentives to keep inventory, a margin anchored to FY2025 is too high. Evidence in rows 7, 23 and 27.
2. **JUDGMENT · AMBER · Outbound growth after the fuel shock.** Management already saw higher fares and weaker ticketing. If expensive or scarce long-haul seats persist, rising border crossings can coexist with slower Trip.com revenue growth. Evidence in rows 17–20.
3. **JUDGMENT · AMBER · Competition for bookings.** Agoda’s H World pact and rivals’ AI booking channels can divert travelers and hotels. Repeated loss of direct bookings can compound over 2033–2042. Evidence in rows 23–27.
4. **JUDGMENT · AMBER · Offshore liquidity.** A note put and maturity can require accessible offshore liquidity. Restricted China balances make the location of cash relevant. Evidence in rows 6, 14 and 29.
5. **JUDGMENT · AMBER · Cash-check denominator.** If the card used the higher cash-flow number from the FY2025 valuation row, the reported 0.80 ratio would understate modeled cash flow relative to audited FY2025 operating cash flow. The denominator used in the production run remains unverified. Evidence in row 9 and the attached company card.
