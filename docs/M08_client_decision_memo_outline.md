# M08 client decision memo: outline

Headings and the points each section needs. The prose goes in
`M08_client_decision_memo.md`, written in my own words, after M07's
results are in. Numbers come from `outputs/M08_contribution_comparison.csv`
and `outputs/M08_2031_ranges.csv`, quoted with their model version.

## 1. The decision

- In 2033 the client sets aside the operating reserve first, then decides
  how much of what is left to give to the facility (case p.3).
- The gift is a choice, not an obligation. Keeping part of the leftover
  preserves flexibility.

## 2. The rule we recommend

- The rule: give a fixed share of the leftover; give nothing if the
  reserve cannot be fully funded.
- Which share, and why it balances the gift against flexibility.
- It was chosen before looking at simulation results (Pass A), then
  tested (Pass B).

## 3. The alternative we rejected

- At least one other share from the comparison, and why it lost.
- If a larger gift was rejected, say which standard it broke.

## 4. What the numbers show

- Payment success: the share of paths that fund all ten payments, against
  the team's certainty target.
- The gift range for each 2031 state, its actual coverage, and how often
  the gift is zero.
- Keep these separate: a likely gift does not mean safe payments.

## 5. Bad markets

- How the range and payment success change under lower returns and lower
  reserve yields.
- What would make the recommendation change.

## 6. Limits

- Results depend on the model and its assumptions. They are not
  predictions.
- The 2031 states are declared examples, not real 2031 balances.
- Taxes, facility cost, and outside funding are out of scope (case p.4).

## 7. Why the payments come first

- The ten operating payments are the one commitment that must be met.
- A gift, once made, can never be pulled back to cover a missed payment.
