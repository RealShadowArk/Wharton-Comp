Prepared 3 November 2026. USD millions unless stated otherwise. No valuation or recommendation is made here.

The clearest new findings

Basin Ranch's table is not an EBITDA range. The first full operating year has forecast EBITDA of approximately $275m and forecast cash flow after senior debt service of approximately $250m. Total construction cost incurred at 30 June 2026 is approximately $600m. Expected total construction cost is $1,800–2,000m; senior financing is approximately $1,100m. The project is 1,350 MW and expected commercial operation is 2029. These are management estimates, not realized earnings. The table identifies the first full operating year without expressly printing a calendar year; do not silently turn that into a guaranteed 2030 cash flow. [K1, board report p.63, section 6B1]

Debt and cash split at 30 June 2026

The board report supplies a management debt table that combines consolidated projects with proportionate interests in associates. It is not the same accounting scope as the consolidated balance sheet. Its cash column includes deposits and restricted cash; its debt includes accrued interest and deferred costs. Do not use the cash column as unrestricted corporate cash.

For reconciliation, the Kenon release reports consolidated OPC borrowings of 2,977 (204 current and 2,773 noncurrent), unrestricted cash of 1,261, restricted cash of 187, and associates' proportionate debt/cash of 642/56. The previously used 2,302 net debt is the arithmetic 2,977 − 1,261 + 642 − 56. It is a different basis from management's 2,057 table, which includes restricted cash, hedge assets and other debt-basis adjustments. Neither should be substituted for the other without reconciliation. [K2, liquidity section]

Other facts for the team's rewrite

• Kenon owns approximately 46% of OPC, including approximately 2% subject to a collar. Kenon's own cash is 512 at 30 June and 605 at 31 August 2026; there is no material debt at Kenon itself. The cash definition includes treasury-management instruments. The September release describes CPV as 70%-owned, rather than the reference memo's approximate 71%; use the dated ownership basis appropriate to the model. [K2]
• The remaining ZIM interest was sold in 2024; the 2025 annual report states that all ZIM shares were sold. No ZIM holding is added to this research. [K3]
• The board declared an approximately 200 cash dividend in March 2026, with April 2026 distribution timing. Payment completion has not been independently verified here; declaration and payment are different facts. [K4]
• The Peru award yielded approximately 93 to Kenon in August 2026 after the capital provider's allocation and certain costs, subject to tax. It is already reflected in the later 605 cash figure; do not add it again. [K2]
• The requested Israel country-risk premium of 1.98% remains unverified. The opened Damodaran webpage is explicitly dated 5 January 2026 and shows 2.07% for Israel, not 1.98%. A dated July 2026 spreadsheet could differ. No replacement premium is selected here. [K5]
• PJM's three delivery-year prices are 329.17/MW-day (2026/27), 333.44 (2027/28) and 325.00 (2028/29). The H1 report identifies the July 2026 result at its price cap. Do not describe all three years as a flat 333/MW-day. [K1, p.19 capacity table; underlying PJM report K6]
• Shay is an initial-stage 2,100 MW West Virginia project. Walker is an initial-stage 1,465 MW Ohio project in the latest table, rather than the older rounded 1,450 MW. They are in the development pipeline, separate from Basin Ranch under construction. These stages are dated to the August 2026 report, not a guarantee of later status. [K1, pp.64–65]
• Secondary reporting says Leviathan and Karish were shut on 28 February 2026. The restart detail is more precise than “about 3 April”: Argus reports clearance for preparations on 31 March and regular Leviathan production resumed on 2 April, announced on 3 April. No primary operator notice was independently verified in this pass. [K7–K8]
